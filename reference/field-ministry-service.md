# Field Ministry Service — Reference Guidelines

> Compiled from jw.org and Watchtower Online Library publications.

---

## A. Midweek Meeting Guidelines (Meetings for Field Service)

These guidelines apply to the midweek meeting arrangement — meetings for field service held before going out in the ministry.

---

### 1. Tips for Making Meetings for Field Service Practical

> Source: Our Christian Life and Ministry—Meeting Workbook, November-December 2021

Meetings for field service are "a provision from Jehovah that enables us to incite one another to love and fine works." These gatherings should run between **5 to 7 minutes**, incorporating group organization, territory assignment, and prayer. When following another meeting, they should be even shorter.

**Purpose and Preparation:**
The conductor should prepare material that has practical application for those participating in ministry that day. "On Saturday when many in attendance have not shared in the ministry since the previous weekend, it may be practical simply to consider what to say."

**Suggested Discussion Topics:**

- Sample conversations from the *Our Christian Life and Ministry—Meeting Workbook*
- Using current events or news reports to initiate conversations
- Responding to common objections
- Engaging with atheists, evolutionists, non-English speakers, or those from uncommon religions
- Utilizing jw.org website features, the JW Library app, or Bible tools
- Resources from the Teaching Toolbox
- Specific ministry approaches including telephone witnessing, letter writing, public witnessing, return visits, and Bible studies
- Reminders covering safety, flexibility, courtesy, and positive attitudes
- Content from the *Apply Yourself to Reading and Teaching* brochure
- Encouraging and supporting field service partners
- Relevant scriptures or encouraging field service experiences

### 2. Meetings for Field Service

> Source: Our Kingdom Ministry, August 2009, pp. 6-7

Meetings for field service are designed to provide "encouragement, practical instruction, and direction" for those engaging in ministry work. The service overseer coordinates these meetings, with group overseers managing specific field service groups.

**Meeting Guidelines:**

- Duration: **10 to 15 minutes** (note: updated to 5-7 minutes in April 2015 — see section 3)
- Hold in convenient locations throughout the congregation's territory rather than at a single location
- Content may briefly discuss the daily text when applicable, review practical resources, and provide territorial assignments

**Attendance Expectations:**

- Participants should arrive punctually
- Understand work assignments before departing
- Experienced publishers are encouraged to mentor newer ones
- Flexibility for occasional reassignments to different partners

**Pioneer Involvement:**

- Pioneers' participation in at least some weekly meetings is valuable
- Their field ministry experience and spiritual maturity serve as examples to others

**Core Objective:**
The arrangement aims to ensure thorough, organized coverage of congregation territories through house-to-house witnessing, following the biblical example of Jesus and his disciples.

### 3. Meetings for Field Service That Accomplish Their Purpose

> Source: Our Kingdom Ministry, March 2015

Meetings for field service serve to "encourage, equip, and organize" those engaging in ministry work, following the example set by Jesus with his 70 disciples (Luke 10).

**Duration Guidelines:**

- Previous standard: 10-15 minutes
- **New standard (April 2015 onwards): 5-7 minutes**
- When following other meetings: even shorter, since attendees have already received scriptural content

**Organizational Approaches:**

Congregations should consider local circumstances when deciding between:

- **Separate group meetings** — easier travel, quicker organization, closer oversight
- **Combined meetings** — various congregational arrangements

**Conductor Responsibilities:**

- Receive assignments in advance
- Prepare thoroughly, despite shorter meeting length
- Obtain territory materials beforehand
- Ensure all participants know assignments before departure
- "The conductor should think about what will encourage and equip those going out in the ministry that day."

**Discussion Topics:**

- Sample presentations
- Applicable videos
- Scripture
- Field service experiences
- Safety reminders
- Practical techniques for specific ministry scenarios

**Publisher Preparation:**

- Prepare during family worship when possible
- Arrive several minutes early
- Obtain materials before meetings begin
- Listen carefully to organizational instructions

### 4. How to Benefit From Your Field Service Group

> Source: Our Kingdom Ministry, October 2012

Field service groups are generally the same size that book study groups were, making them suitable for fostering close friendships and personalized spiritual support.

**Building Friendships:**

- Working alongside others in ministry "side by side" can create meaningful bonds
- Expand your circle by working with different group members
- Occasionally invite group members to family gatherings or meals

**Receiving Shepherding:**

- Group overseers provide personal encouragement and ministry training
- The service overseer also participates with different groups monthly, offering additional mentorship opportunities

**Organizing Meetings:**

- Separate group meetings allow for efficient organization and quick departure to territories
- Combined meetings work when necessary, with group overseers given time to organize their members

**Offering Your Home:**

- Members are encouraged to consider hosting field service meetings
- "Meetings for field service are an extension of the congregation arrangement"
- Home size doesn't matter — elders consider location and other practical factors

**Key Principle:**
"Jehovah continues to provide everything we need" — active participation maximizes available blessings.

### 5. Part 3—A Center for Field Ministry

> Source: Our Kingdom Ministry, September 1991, p. 4

This article addresses how congregation book study locations can serve as organizational centers for field ministry activities.

**Purpose and Organization:**

- Book study locations should "serve as a meeting place for field service"
- Scheduling should accommodate most publishers
- Provide practical assistance for organizing evangelism work

**Conductor Responsibilities:**

- The book study conductor leads organizational efforts
- "Is responsible for organizing the activity of the group"
- Works directly with publishers in the field
- Prepares meetings that offer "Scriptural and practical instruction"

**Consistency and Participation:**

- Regular scheduling at fixed times and locations encourages consistent involvement
- Publishers benefit from knowing "there will be territory available and others to work with"

**Training and Support:**

- The conductor can arrange for experienced publishers to mentor others
- Those providing assistance should maintain "a positive and balanced view"
- Offer "commendation as progress is made"

**Broader Goal:**
Mutual encouragement among group members to become "effective in preaching the good news and making disciples" while supporting one another through shared ministry work.
